01Overview

One window. Nothing to arrange.

The Chrome extension lines up separate Chrome windows. The Mac app keeps them all in one window instead, split into panes. Move the window, make it full screen, put it on another display: the deck goes with it.

Spotlight

Click to bring one forward

Click a pane's title bar and it grows to most of the window. The others stay live on a rail at the side, so you can keep an eye on them. Click again, or press ⌥Z, to go back to the grid.

⌥X ⌥Z ⌥Q

Every shortcut, already set

⌥X spotlight, ⌥Z grid, ⌥Q fill the window, ⌥1–8 jump to a pane, ⌥] and ⌥[ cycle, ⌥B the sidebar. They work even while a page has the keyboard.

Import

Bring your deck along

Already use the extension? Save a backup there and open it in the app. Your windows, names and URLs come across, and the ones on your active layout start visible.

04Install

Four steps, about a minute.

The app is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so macOS opens it after one ordinary confirmation.

  1. Download AI-Window-Deck-mac.dmg and open it.
  2. Drag AI Window Deck into Applications.
  3. Open it from Applications. When macOS says it was downloaded from the internet, choose Open.
  4. Optional: in the extension, save a Backup, then choose Import from extension in the app's sidebar.

Requirements

  • macOS 12 Monterey or later
  • Apple silicon or Intel: one universal app
  • About 210 MB to download

Past versions and release notes are on GitHub Releases.

05Privacy

Your sessions stay on your Mac.

No account, no analytics, no server of our own. The app talks to the sites you open in it, and to GitHub to look for updates.

  • Your windows and settings are saved in a file on your Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/AI Window Deck.
  • Sign-ins and cookies from the sites in your panes are kept by the app, separate from Chrome or Safari.
  • About every six hours the app downloads a small file from GitHub Releases to see if there is a newer version. Nothing about you or your windows is sent.
  • No analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting to the developer.
  • A page can use the microphone or camera only after macOS asks you, as in any browser.

06FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do I need the Chrome extension too?
No. The Mac app works on its own. Use either one, or both: the extension for Chrome windows across several displays, the app when you want one window.
Why isn't it on the Mac App Store?
The App Store requires a sandbox that would get in the way of running full web apps side by side. The app is still signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, which means Apple has checked it for malicious software.
Will I have to sign in to every site again?
Once, inside the app. The app keeps its own sign-ins, separate from Chrome, and all panes share them, so signing in to a site in one pane signs in every pane that shows it.
How do updates work?
The app checks for a new version when it starts and about every six hours. It downloads in the background, then a button in the title bar offers to restart. If you skip it, the update installs the next time you quit. Check for Updates… in the app menu checks right away.
What happens to the price after the beta?
Beta versions stay free to use. Version 1.0 is planned as a one-time purchase, and the price will be announced here before it launches. The Chrome extension stays free.
Is there a Windows version?
Not yet. The app is built so that one can follow, but the Mac version comes first.
How do I uninstall it?
Quit the app and move it from Applications to the Trash. To remove your windows and sign-ins as well, delete ~/Library/Application Support/AI Window Deck.
